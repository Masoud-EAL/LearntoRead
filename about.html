About WordLink

Free reading and phonics practice for adults learning English

For learners

WordLink helps you learn to read English. It is free. You do not need to buy anything or make an account.

You can start with the letters. Tap a letter and you will hear it. Then you can learn the sound each letter makes, and read short words, and read sentences. You can practise speaking too.

Every word has a picture and a voice. You can listen slowly, or faster when you are ready. You can hear the words in your first language as well — Dari, Vietnamese, Chinese, Urdu, Khmer, Burmese, Hindi, Arabic or Thai.

You can use it on your phone. Take your time. Go back as many times as you like.

For teachers and tutors

WordLink is a free, browser-based phonics and reading resource for adult EAL learners at pre-level — adults with little or no prior literacy in any language, learning to read and write English for the first time. It was built by an EAL teacher for use in adult classes, and it is deliberately not a children's app: no mascots, no cartoons, no condescending language.

What it covers

  • The alphabet — letter names, with audio on every letter.
  • Letter sounds — each grapheme in the context of real words, including second sounds for the letters that have them.
  • Reading words 1 — CVC decoding, short vowels and digraphs, with tappable letter tiles so a learner can sound out a word piece by piece.
  • Reading words 2 — consonant blends and common English spelling patterns.
  • Reading sentences — two levels, beginner and elementary.
  • Speaking practice — the learner records an answer and compares it with a model.
  • Letter tracing — stroke-order handwriting practice for letters, letter combinations, numbers and the learner's own name.
  • Spelling and pronunciation practice and a test yourself mode inside each section.

Using it in class

Audio plays at three speeds, so the same material works for a learner who needs every phoneme separated and for one who is ready for connected speech. First-language glosses can be switched on or off per learner, which is useful in the mixed-first-language classrooms typical of AMEP and other adult migrant English settings.

There is also a classroom game mode. You create a room on the projector, students join on their phones with a room code or a QR code, and the class answers together — or a learner can practise solo at their own pace. You can write your own question banks for it.

Nothing needs installing. It runs in a browser, works on cheap phones, and keeps working offline once a learner has opened it, which matters when data is scarce.

Where it fits

The content sits at roughly ACSF pre-level to Level 1 for reading and writing, which makes it useful in CIEAL, CEAL and Course in Initial EAL classes, and in the early stages of general adult EAL and literacy programs. It is designed as supplementary practice — something a learner can repeat between classes — rather than a course in itself, and it is not mapped or accredited against any curriculum.

WordLink is an independent project made by an EAL teacher. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or published by AMES Australia, the Adult Migrant English Program, or any other provider. Any course names above are referred to only to describe the level the material suits.

Cost, accounts and privacy

WordLink is free and has no advertising. There is no sign-up and no account for the learning activities — progress is remembered on the device itself. The classroom game signs players in anonymously so that a room can keep score during a session; no names beyond the nickname a student types are collected.

Visitor numbers are counted with a privacy-friendly analytics service that does not use cookies or track people between sites.
