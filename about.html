About WordLink

Free reading and phonics practice for adults learning English

For learners

WordLink helps you learn to read English. It is free. You do not need to buy anything or make an account.

You can start with the letters. Tap a letter and you will hear it. Then you can learn the sound each letter makes, and read short words, and read sentences. You can practise speaking too.

Every word has a picture and a voice. You can listen slowly, or faster when you are ready. You can hear the words in your first language as well — Dari, Persian, Pashto, Vietnamese, Chinese, Urdu, Khmer, Burmese, Hindi, Arabic or Thai.

You can use it on your phone. Take your time. Go back as many times as you like.

For teachers and tutors

WordLink is a free, browser-based phonics and reading resource for adult EAL learners at pre-level — adults with little or no prior literacy in any language, learning to read and write English for the first time. It was built by an EAL teacher for use in adult classes, and it is deliberately not a children's app: no mascots, no cartoons, no condescending language.

What it covers

  • The alphabet — letter names, with audio on every letter.
  • Letter sounds — each grapheme in the context of real words, including second sounds for the letters that have them.
  • Reading words 1 — CVC decoding, short vowels and digraphs, with tappable letter tiles so a learner can sound out a word piece by piece.
  • Reading words 2 — consonant blends and common English spelling patterns.
  • Reading sentences — two levels, beginner and elementary.
  • Speaking practice — the learner records an answer and compares it with a model.
  • Letter tracing — stroke-order handwriting practice for letters, letter combinations, numbers and the learner's own name.
  • Spelling and pronunciation practice and a test yourself mode inside each section.

Every game and activity

Each one opens as its own page and works solo on a phone. The games can also be played with a whole class in the Teacher Game, along with any question banks you write yourself.

Writing and tracing

  • ✍️ Trace the Letters with Your Finger: The learner watches a letter being written, then traces it with a finger from the start dot. Capital and small letters are both there, formed the right way from the first try.
  • 📖 Tracing Words: For learners who can already trace single letters. Short picture words like cat, sun and cup are traced left to right, so the letters connect to a meaning.
  • 🙋 Trace Your Name in English: The learner types their name and traces it one letter at a time, with the capital at the start drawn correctly. The phone remembers the name for next time.
  • 🔢 Trace the Numbers with Your Finger: Hear a number name, watch it written, then trace it from the start dot. Covers all ten digits from 0 to 9, ready for phone numbers, dates and amounts on forms.

Getting started with reading

  • 🔊 Sound Words: A guide to how WordLink teaches decoding: letter sounds first, then Words 1, then the longer words in Words 2. Any word can be tapped to hear it read aloud.
  • 📚 Reading Games for Adults: An overview of the reading games, from letters and sounds through sight words to signs, notices, messages and forms. Useful when choosing games for a class.

Letters and Sounds

  • 🔤 Letter Names: Hear a letter name and tap it from four, in printed or handwritten letters. Letters that sound alike, such as B, D, P and V, come up often.
  • 🔠 Big and Small Letters: Find the small partner of a capital letter, or the capital of a small one. Writing rounds turn a name like fatima into Fatima.
  • 🔡 Beginner Sound Gap: Hear a short word like cat and tap the missing first sound in _at. Each round stays in one rhyming family, from -an to -un.
  • 👆 Beginner Tap Word: Hear a word like pen and pick it from pen, hen, ten and men. Only the first sound changes, so the learner must read it.
  • 🧱 Beginner Build Word: Put sound tiles in order to spell a word you hear, like m, a, n for man. Every tile plays its own sound in a real voice.
  • 🔡 Elementary Sound Gap: Tap the missing two-letter sound in everyday words like ship, phone and train. Covers sh, ch, th and vowel teams such as ee, ai and oa.
  • 👆 Elementary Tap Word: Hear a word and choose it from four with the same pattern, like moon, food, soon and pool. Reading the whole word is the only way to be sure.
  • 🧱 Elementary Build Word: Build words from tiles where two letters make one sound: ship is sh, i, p and train is t, r, ai, n. A key step towards spelling longer words.

Listening

  • 📝 Beginner Listen and Fill Gaps: Hear a short sentence about someone, like My ___ is David, and type or tap the missing word. The sentence can be played again.
  • 📄 Elementary Listen and Fill Gaps: Hear a longer sentence and fill the gap. The sentences use the elementary sound patterns, so listening supports phonics.
  • 🎧 Listen and Unscramble: Hear a sentence about family, home or the weekend, then put the word tiles in order. In multiple choice, tap the correct sentence.
  • 👋 Greetings: Hear a greeting or a simple question, like How are you? or Where are you from?, and tap the right reply. Social English for the first weeks.
  • 🖐️ Do What It Says: Hear a short instruction, like Open the door or Take a photo, and tap the matching picture. Covers classroom and phone instructions.

Reading

  • 👁️ Sight Words: Hear a common word like the, was or come and tap it from four. Many of these words cannot be sounded out, so they need to be known on sight.
  • 🖼️ Picture and Word: Look at a picture, such as a bus or an apple, and tap its word from four. The instruction is spoken and there is no typing.
  • 📢 Read the Notice: Read a short notice, like a shop closed sign or a bus timetable, and answer what, when or how much. Practises finding the key detail.

Writing

  • 📝 Copy It: Copy a word, a full name like Mai Nguyen, or a number. Capitals have to match, the same way a form expects a name to be written.
  • ❕ Capitals and Full Stops: Choose the sentence that is written correctly, or rewrite one with a capital letter and a full stop. Everyday sentences like I live in Springvale.
  • ✏️ Write a Sentence: Follow two model sentences to write two of your own, about your name, country, home, family or work. The capital letters and full stops are checked too.
  • 🗂️ All Spelling: A mixed spelling round from every topic: number words, days, months, weather, fruit, body and clothes. Multiple choice makes it easier.

Speaking

  • 🗣️ Speaking Game: Hear a question like Where do you live? and build the answer from word tiles. The answer is then read aloud so the learner can say it too.

Grammar

  • ❓ Question Words: Read a question with its first word missing, like ___ is your name?, and choose the question word. The questions asked at enrolment, the doctor and work.
  • 🔀 Grammar Unscramble: Put the mixed-up words of a written sentence in order, with no audio. The sentences are about yourself, your family and your day.

Vocabulary

  • 📅 Days of the Week: Hear a day of the week and type it, capital letter included. Learners who cannot type yet can tap instead.
  • 🗓️ Months: Hear a month and type it with a capital letter. February, August and September usually need the most practice.
  • 🌦️ Weather: Hear and spell words like sunny, windy, cold and humid, or tap the matching picture. Words for small talk and the weather forecast.
  • 🍎 Fruits: Hear and spell fruit names from apple to watermelon, or tap the picture in multiple choice. Handy words for the shops.
  • 👤 Parts of the Body: Hear and spell body words from head to toe, with clear drawings. The words for telling a doctor where it hurts.
  • 👕 Clothes: Hear and spell clothes and accessories, with drawings. Includes Australian words like jumper, thongs and beanie.
  • 🧩 Odd One Out: Look at four pictures and tap the one that is not from the same group, like animals, transport or clothes. Builds vocabulary by meaning.

Numbers

  • 🔢 Numbers 1 to 10: Hear a number from one to ten and type its word. Learners who cannot type yet can tap instead.
  • 🔢 Numbers 11 to 20: Hear a number from eleven to twenty and type its word. The teen numbers, like twelve, fifteen and eighteen, are the hard ones.
  • 🔢 Numbers 21 to 99: Hear a number like forty-two and spell it, hyphen included. Needed for writing amounts in words.
  • 🔢 Tap the Number: Hear a number and tap it from four close choices: one more, one less, ten more or the digits swapped. Careful listening, no typing.
  • ✍️ Write the Number: Write numbers and money in figures: a number you hear or see, the value of an Australian coin or note, or an amount like $4.50.
  • ➕ Add It Up: Hear and read a simple sum, like four plus three, and type the answer. The maths stays easy so the English is the focus.
  • 💵 Add the Money: Add two amounts in whole dollars, like $5 and $3. The question is spoken and shown, like working out a total at the shops.
  • ↕️ Put In Order: Put numbers, Australian coins and notes, three days or three months in order. Tap or drag the tiles.
  • 🔟 Tens and Ones: See a number from 11 to 99 and answer how many tens or how many ones. Every question is read aloud and shown on screen.
  • 📊 Read the List: Read a price list for everyday groceries and answer how much, which is cheaper, or which is more expensive.
  • 📆 Find the Date: Read a real month calendar to find what day a date falls on, or which month it shows. Practice for appointments and due dates.
  • 🥇 First, Second, Third: Hear first, second or third and tap the right person in a row, or name a marked position. The words for queues and floors.
  • 🏅 Ordinal Numbers: Ordinals from first to thirtieth, the way dates are said. The wrong choices are the usual mix-ups, like thirteenth and thirtieth.
  • 🧭 Where Is It: Say which way an arrow points, up, down, left or right, or whether a box is in front of or behind a person.
  • ⏰ Clock: Read an analogue clock, then drag its hands to set a time. It moves from o'clock and half past to quarter past, quarter to and 5-minute steps.
  • 🕐 Digital Time: Hear a time and tap or type it in numbers, as on a phone or appointment card. Includes quarter hours and the difference between AM and PM.
  • 🛒 Shop: Pay for everyday items with real Australian coins and notes, from 5c to $50, and work out the change.
  • 🎲 Dice: Roll two dice, add them to your score and choose to roll again or stop. The aim is to get closest to the target without going over.
  • 🗺️ Directions: Hear and read directions like Turn left at the bank, then draw the way on a street map. Words like next to and opposite find the place.

Translation

  • 🌐 Translate: Read a sentence in your first language, such as Arabic, Dari or Vietnamese, and write it in English. For learners who can read their own language.

Using a device

  • 💻 Devices: Name everyday devices like a printer, charger or headphones, and say what each one is for.
  • 🔣 Screen Symbols: See an icon, like search, settings, back or delete, and tap its name. The symbols on phones, computers and kiosks.
  • ⚠️ What Should You Do?: Choose what to do about a phone message, like battery low or storage full, or a call from an unknown number. Also what to try when the phone will not turn on.
  • 🔒 Security: Decide if something is safe to share, like a first name, or must stay private, like a PIN or passport photo. Other rounds copy a 4-digit SMS code.

Everyday English

  • 🚪 Signs: See a public or safety sign with no words on it, like wet floor or no parking, and tap what it means from four choices.
  • 📱 Messages: Read real text messages, like an appointment reminder or a shopping request from family, and answer what day, what time, who or what to bring.
  • 📋 Forms: Put a person's details into the right boxes on a form, from family name to postcode. Other rounds read the name or number on an ID card.

Mixed practice

  • ✨ All Games: One mixed round drawn from every game. A quick review across all skills.

Test yourself and Level Check

  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Ten rounds from every game that get harder or easier with each answer. The results show strong skills and which games to practise next.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Letters and Sounds: An adaptive check of the alphabet and all six phonics games. The results point to the phonics games to practise next.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Vocabulary: An adaptive check across every vocabulary topic, plus sight words, devices and question words.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Listening: An adaptive check of how well a learner understands spoken letters, words, sentences, times and instructions.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Speaking: An adaptive check of the language behind speaking: answering questions, choosing question words, replying to greetings and ordering a sentence.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Reading: An adaptive reading check that moves from sight words and pictures up to signs, messages, notices, forms and price lists.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Writing: An adaptive check that runs from copying words and names up to writing sentences from a model.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Grammar: An adaptive check of question words, sentence word order, capitals and full stops.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Numbers: An adaptive check of numbers, money, time, dates and price lists, all asked in English.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Translation: An adaptive check in eleven languages: read a sentence in your own language and write or choose the English.
  • 🎯 Test Yourself: Everyday English: An adaptive check of survival English: signs, messages, forms, time, money and staying safe online.
  • 📋 Level Check: A placement check for teachers and tutors. It ends with an indicative ACSF level, from Pre Level 1 Stage A to Level 1, and a report of strengths and gaps to confirm in an interview.

In the game itself these are filtered by skill — Letters & Sounds, Vocabulary, Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing, Grammar, Numeracy, Translation and Everyday life — so a bank that trains several skills at once can be found under any of them.

Using it in class

Audio plays at three speeds, so the same material works for a learner who needs every phoneme separated and for one who is ready for connected speech. First-language glosses can be switched on or off per learner, which is useful in the mixed-first-language classrooms typical of AMEP and other adult migrant English settings.

There is also a classroom game mode. You create a room on the projector, students join on their phones with a room code or a QR code, and the class answers together — or a learner can practise solo at their own pace. You can write your own question banks for it.

Nothing needs installing. It runs in a browser, works on cheap phones, and keeps working offline once a learner has opened it, which matters when data is scarce.

It is worth installing it on learners' phones in the first lesson, with the steps in Install the WordLink app on your phone above: WordLink then opens from its own icon, full screen, like any other app, and a learner who cannot yet type a web address can still find it again at home. Share the address wordlink.au or the room QR code from the game, and walk the class through the three steps together on the projector.

Ways to use it in a lesson

  • Differentiate: point each learner at the section that fits them: the alphabet for a true beginner, Reading words 2 or the elementary sentences for a stronger reader.
  • Stations and rotations: learners work on their phones independently while you take a small group.
  • Diagnose: the Level check, Reading words 2 and the elementary sentences show quickly which sound patterns a learner has not got yet.
  • Homework and self-study: any section can be repeated between classes, as often as a learner likes.
  • Whole-class review: run the classroom game from the projector at the end of a lesson on the patterns you just taught.

Where it fits

The content sits at roughly ACSF pre-level to Level 1 for reading and writing, which makes it useful in Course in Initial EAL (CIEAL) and CEAL classes, and in the early stages of general adult EAL and literacy programs. It is designed as supplementary practice — something a learner can repeat between classes — rather than a course in itself, and it is not mapped or accredited against any curriculum.

WordLink is an independent project made by an EAL teacher. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or published by AMES Australia, the Adult Migrant English Program, or any other provider. Any course names above are referred to only to describe the level the material suits.

Cost, accounts and privacy

WordLink is free and has no advertising. There is no sign-up and no account for the learning activities — progress is remembered on the device itself. The classroom game signs players in anonymously so that a room can keep score during a session; no names beyond the nickname a student types are collected.

Visitor numbers are counted with a privacy-friendly analytics service that does not use cookies or track people between sites.
