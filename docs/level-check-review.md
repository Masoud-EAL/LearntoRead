# Level Check against ACSF Pre Level 1 (2017)

How well the Level Check in `game.html` can be used to assess Pre Level 1A (PL1A) and Pre Level 1B (PL1B), read against the official *ACSF Pre Level 1* document (Commonwealth of Australia, 2017), and what must change.

## 1. Overall verdict

- **Usable as an indicative screen, not as an assessment.** The run produces an honest, hedged per-indicator profile (NYA / PL1A / PL1B) that mirrors the way the ACSF reports a learner (one stage per indicator, as in the Binh and Sharlene profiles). A teacher can start an assessor analysis sheet from it. The panel already says this.
- **The design is sound: the content has drift.** The per-indicator staircase, the "asked and not shown" versus "never asked" distinction, the refusal to award a stage skipped below, and the exclusion of speaking (.07) and handwriting are all correct against the framework. The problems are in individual items and claims, listed in sections 3 and 4.
- **Two structural issues limit validity for PL1A specifically:** nearly every non-reading item is answered by reading written options, and every item is timed. Both push down learners at the very band the check exists to find.
- **Reference mismatch.** The app quotes the SEE reporting checklist's *key* features, not the 2017 document. The 2017 document has more features per stage (PL1A .04 lists twelve; the app can reach six) and different indicator titles. This is acceptable if the panel says it is a sample, but the titles should match the document (section 4.1).

## 2. What the check gets right

- **One stage per indicator.** A learner can come out PL1B in numeracy and NYA in reading, which is what the ACSF profile figures show and what a single global band cannot.
- **Exit-level logic.** A stage is judged only after every feature the pool can ask has been asked, and PL1B is never asked or credited before PL1A is shown. This matches "sufficient evidence at PL1A" as the condition for exit level PL1A.
- **NYA is reported as NYA**, with "it was asked, nothing here says the learner cannot do it on another day", which is the right register for this audience.
- **Honest exclusions.** .07 is "not assessable by this app"; every handwriting feature (holds pencil, writes on line, tops and tails) is left unclaimed; Level 1 rows are always partial.
- **Repetition is allowed.** "Hear it again" matches the PL1A feature "relies heavily on repetition and rephrasing for understanding" rather than penalising it.
- **Features are quoted verbatim** on the teacher panel, so a teacher can tick the sheet from the app's wording.

## 3. Structural issues that must be fixed

### 3.1 Reading demand inside non-reading items

- Listening (.08), numeracy (.09 to .11) and digital (.12, .13) items are multiple choice with **written** options and no audio on the options. Examples: "See you next week" with the options *Goodbye / Please / Good morning / Yes, of course*; "Which way does the arrow point?" with *left / behind / up / right*; "What is this called?" with *headphones / phone / camera / mouse*.
- A PL1A learner by definition cannot read these options. A wrong answer is then recorded against listening or numeracy when the failure was reading. The check confounds every indicator with .03/.04.
- **Change:** in the level check, options for .08, .09 to .11, .12 and .13 must be pictures, numerals, or spoken on tap. Where a written option is unavoidable, tap-to-hear on each option.

### 3.2 The timer contradicts the Performance Variables Grid

- The grid for PL1 (both stages) says the learner "works with an expert/mentor where highly structured support and modelling is provided, initiated by the expert/mentor". The document's own scenario notes that Binh "worked slowly and carefully and needed constant reassurance".
- The level check runs a clock on every item (40 s base; 80 s typed; 120 s for two sentences) and counts timeouts as "no attempt" in the Learning signals for .01 and .02. A slow learner therefore loses Learning credit for a property of the band, not of the learner.
- **Change:** no visible clock in the level check, or a much longer hidden ceiling, and timeouts must not count against .01/.02. The panel should tell the teacher to sit beside the learner, prompt, and re-read, which the grid expects.

### 3.3 One item per feature

- Most features are asked exactly once, and a stage needs 75 percent of its evidence right. For a three-feature stage (PL1A .03 is Letter Names, Signs, Forms) that means three of three, so one slip closes the indicator at NYA. Conversely, three four-option taps can be passed by guessing.
- **Change:** ask each PL1A feature twice and judge at feature level (a feature is shown if either attempt is right, or on two of three). This costs about ten questions for a PL1A learner and gives the NYA/PL1A line, the one this check is for, real evidence.

## 4. Parts, sections and questions that must change

### 4.1 Labels and titles

- Indicator titles on the panel are the Level 1 titles ("Demonstrates some awareness of self as a learner", "Identifies personally relevant information and ideas from texts on highly familiar topics"). The 2017 document's Pre Level 1 titles are different ("Demonstrates extremely limited awareness of self as a learner", "Makes limited meaning from extremely simple and familiar written and pictorial text"). Print the Pre Level 1 titles from Table 1.
- The document uses **PL1A / PL1B**, "exit level" and "NYA". The app prints PLA / PLB. Rename on the panel and in the copied text.
- .12 and .13 do not exist in the ACSF Pre Level 1 document. Keep them if the assessor sheet needs them, but label them as Digital Literacy Skills Framework rows so no one reads them as ACSF.

### 4.2 Learning (.01, .02)

- PL1B .02 is awarded to anyone who answers at least twelve questions with half of them attempted, regardless of correctness. PL1A/PL1B .01 is nearly the same. These rows carry no discriminating information and should be shown as observations, not as a stage award, or the panel should say plainly that they never place a learner.
- "Begins to express extremely simple learning goal" (PL1A .01) is credited from the act of attempting questions. The My Learning goal question already exists and is the natural evidence; ask it at PL1A and treat "No goal" as not shown.

### 4.3 Reading (.03, .04)

- **Missing PL1A feature:** "Recognises a small bank of extremely familiar words supported by pictures" (.04) is never asked. The clothes, fruit and body banks claim it but are not in the pool. This is the most characteristic PL1A reading task in the document (the Binh scenario is picture-to-word matching). Add it.
- **Which Comes First** ("Which word is first?") gives its answer away: the first word keeps its capital (*I*, *My*) and the others are lower case. Show the sentence in one case, or observe the feature differently.
- **Question Words** deals auxiliary items (*Can / Are / Do / Is*, *Is / Are / Do / Does*). Those are not "frequently used question words, e.g. who, what" and are above PL1B. Restrict the level-check draw to wh-words.
- PL1B .03 and .04 are otherwise well covered (Messages, beginner sentences, Signs, Forms, Tap the Word, Odd One Out).

### 4.4 Writing (.05, .06)

- **Own name.** "Copies first and last name" (PL1A) and "Writes full name accurately" (PL1B .06) are credited from copying a stranger's name (*Fatima Ahmadi*). The features mean the learner's own name, and "writes" means without a model. Let the teacher enter the learner's name at the start; PL1A copies it, PL1B writes it with the model hidden.
- "Writes limited personal details" (PL1B .05) is also credited from the same copy-a-name round. In the level check the Forms round is drag-and-drop chips, so nothing personal is written. Either add a typed Forms round to the pool or drop the claim.
- **Spelling bank is above the band.** The dictated words include *humid, beanie, wrist, necklace, stomach, watermelon, pineapple*. PL1 features say "extremely familiar" and "highly familiar" words. Use a short fixed list (name, bus, stop, shop, milk, exit) for the level check.
- Dictated spelling is credited to PL1A .06 "**Copies** ... but may have spelling inaccuracies". Dictation is harder than copying. Leave PL1A copying to Copy It and keep Spelling for PL1B "writes a very limited number of extremely familiar words".
- **Unclaimed evidence the document gives you.** The 2017 document lists "Uses keypad to key in single letters or numbers" (PL1A .05, .06) and "Uses keypad or phone to key in extremely simple words" (PL1B .05, .06). Every typed answer shows these directly. Claim them; it removes the need to apologise for keyboard-only writing.
- Write Two Sentences is marked well (two sentences, capitals, stops, a copied model scores partial). Keep as is.

### 4.5 Listening (.08)

- PL1A "Understands basic one word greetings" and "social exchanges using single words" are tested with full-sentence exchanges and full-sentence written options. Add single-word items with picture or spoken options (hello, goodbye, thank you, sit, stop).
- "Understands a very limited bank of extremely familiar words" is credited from Letter Names. Letter names are not words. Use a heard word matched to a picture.
- PL1A "Understands extremely short and highly explicit questions" and "requests for basic information" are never asked (the Speaking bank is excluded from the pool). Add a listen-and-tap item: hear "What is your name?" and tap the picture or short answer.

### 4.6 Numeracy (.09, .10, .11)

- **Position words at PL1A** deal *behind, right, left, in front*. PL1A is "up, down" only; left, right, in front and behind are PL1B. Restrict the PL1A draw.
- **Cents at PL1A.** Write the Number at stage A dictates "6 dollars and 50 cents"; Read the List at stage A shows $1.50, $2.50 and $10.50. The PL1A features say **whole dollar** amounts **up to $10**. Restrict both.
- **Digital time** typed items deal "6:15 am" and "ten forty-five" against the PL1B feature "digital time in whole hours". Restrict to whole hours.
- **Naming coins.** "Names Australian coins and notes up to $10" is credited from paying for an item, where nothing is named. Add a "which coin is this?" item with spoken options, or drop the claim.
- **.11 oral language rows** ("uses personally relevant informal language and gestures to convey...") are credited from a recognition task. These are productive features, like .07, and cannot be shown on screen. Mark them not assessable rather than awarding them.
- **Copy versus write at PL1A .11.** The document says "Copies numbers from 0 to 10"; the app credits it from dictation ("Write this number: ten"). Use Copy the Number for PL1A and dictation for PL1B "Writes numbers from 0 to 100".
- **Missing PL1A features worth adding:** "Matches number of objects with number symbols up to 10" and "Counts forwards by 1 to 10" (count the pictures, tap the numeral). These are the sample activities the document gives for PL1A and the app has nothing for them. "Oral day markers (yesterday, today, tomorrow)" is also absent.
- Add It Up (no carrying), Put In Order, Tens and Ones, ordinals 1st to 3rd, calendar and Tap the Number are well matched to the document at both stages.

## 5. Priority list

1. **Must:** spoken or pictorial options on all .08 to .13 items in the level check (3.1).
2. **Must:** remove the clock from the level check and stop counting timeouts against Learning (3.2).
3. **Must:** fix the item-level overclaims: own name, spelling list, position words, cents, digital time, coin naming, .11 oral rows, question-word auxiliaries, first-word capital (4.3 to 4.6).
4. **Should:** add the missing PL1A items: picture-word matching, count the objects, single-word greetings, short spoken questions (4.3, 4.5, 4.6).
5. **Should:** two attempts per PL1A feature and feature-level judging (3.3).
6. **Should:** print the 2017 indicator titles and the PL1A / PL1B / NYA labels; mark .12 and .13 as non-ACSF (4.1).
7. **Could:** claim the keypad features the document already provides (4.4); show .01/.02 as observations only (4.2).
